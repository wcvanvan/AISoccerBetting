## Caveats

1. **Unprecedented tactical situation**: A team chasing a 3-0 UCL deficit at home is rare. Historical data may underestimate the corner uplift from this level of desperation. If my City estimate is too conservative by even 1.5 corners, the Under 10.5 and City U9.5 picks lose their edge (Under 11.5 remains robust up to λ≈12.0).

2. **Mbappé and Bellingham squad return**: Both are back in Madrid's 26-man squad after injury. If either starts — especially Mbappé — Madrid's tactical approach could shift from purely defensive to active counter-pressing, increasing both Madrid's corner output and the match total by 1-2 corners. The predicted lineup has them on the bench, but this is a significant risk factor.

3. **First-leg anchoring**: The first leg (City 10 corners, Madrid 1) is the most relevant data point but is also a single game. Over-weighting it risks anchoring to an outlier. Removing it from the H2H average drops the corner total from 10.0 to 9.67.

4. **Small venue-filtered samples**: City home (n=9) and the single H2H at the Etihad (n=1, from Feb 2025 with only 3/11 starter overlap) are thin. The statistical confidence intervals around my estimates are wide.

5. **Sub-Poisson variance**: The observed variance (2.62) is far below the Poisson assumption (variance = mean = 10.5). My Poisson model is conservative for Under bets — the true Under probabilities are likely *higher* than stated. This strengthens the case for all three picks but means my probability estimates should be treated as floor estimates.

6. **Score-state sensitivity**: If City score in the first 15 minutes, the match dynamics shift entirely — Madrid may need to push forward, increasing their corners and the total. Conversely, if Madrid score on the counter, the tie is dead and corner patterns become unpredictable.

7. **Model assumption of independence**: The Poisson model treats each team's corners as independent. In practice, open games inflate both teams' corners (positive correlation), which could increase total variance and make Over outcomes more likely than the model predicts. However, with Madrid defending deep, the game is unlikely to be "open" — one-directional territory favours negative correlation (more City corners, fewer Madrid corners), which actually supports the spread picks.

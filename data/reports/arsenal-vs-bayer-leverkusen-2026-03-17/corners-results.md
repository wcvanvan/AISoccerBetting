## Corner Patterns

- **Arsenal generation**: Most corners came from sustained territorial pressure on the right — Saka winning the byline or having crosses blocked. Three Arsenal corners arrived in a 5-minute cluster (16'–21') and another two back-to-back at 28'–29', reflecting a full-throated first-half siege.
- **Leverkusen generation**: Mostly opportunistic from blocked clearances; only the late 73'/74' sequence came from genuine attacking pressure (Leverkusen finally entering Arsenal's third with regularity).
- **Delivery patterns**: Saka was Arsenal's primary corner taker, mixing inswingers to Gabriel's near-post run with a deliberate short corner at 28' that produced the match's most dangerous set-piece sequence (two saves from Blaswich). Rice took the secondary deliveries. Leverkusen funnelled all 8 of their corners through Grimaldo (left foot, predominantly outswinging from the left), with Garcia covering the right side once.
- **Dangerous vs routine**: 5 dangerous Arsenal corners (9', 21', 28', 63'-area), most targeting Gabriel/Saliba-level aerial threat — but only the 28' short routine produced shots on target. Leverkusen's 73' corner was their only genuinely threatening set piece, ironically nearly producing an own goal.
- **Game-state effect**: Arsenal's corner cluster (9'–33') came at 0–0 as they pushed for the opener; Leverkusen's late corner cluster (73', 74', 74', 90+3') came at 0–2 chasing the aggregate. Neither cluster converted.
- **Set-piece partnerships**: Saka → Gabriel near-post run was the recurring combination (9', 21'), continuing Arsenal's season-long template. Saka → Trossard short variation surfaced once (28').

## Notable Observations

- Arsenal entered the tie with 16 Premier League corner-kick goals and 20+ set-piece goals season-wide; Leverkusen had publicly conceded set-piece avoidance was a tactical priority after the first leg, when Andrich himself scored from a corner against Arsenal.
- Defender Piero Hincapié pre-match: "Our set-piece strength is talked about all over Europe. That's the result of daily training with our set-piece coach, Nico Jover."
- Despite the first-leg corner concession driving Leverkusen's defensive plan, Arsenal generated 10 corners and forced Blaswich into two of his 10 saves directly from the 28' short routine — the closest a corner came to producing a goal in this leg.

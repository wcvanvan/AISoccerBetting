### Spreads Market

**Alavés −0.5 corners (DK 1.83)** — Alavés wins the corner count outright.

Using Skellam distribution for the difference D = A − B, where A ~ Poisson(5.0) and B ~ Poisson(2.8):
- E[D] = 5.0 − 2.8 = **2.2**
- Var[D] = 5.0 + 2.8 = **7.8** → SD = 2.79

P(D ≥ 1) i.e. Alavés wins corner count:
Normal approximation with continuity correction: P(Z > (0.5 − 2.2)/2.79) = P(Z > −0.61) = Φ(0.61) ≈ **73%**.

Sensitivity test with conservative inputs (A=4.5, B=3.5):
E[D] = 1.0, Var = 8.0, SD = 2.83. P(D ≥ 1) = P(Z > (0.5−1.0)/2.83) = P(Z > −0.18) = Φ(0.18) ≈ **57%**.

Even with λA = 4.5 and λB = 3.5 — an aggressively pessimistic scenario for Alavés and generous for Villarreal — the probability still slightly exceeds the book's implied 54.6%.

Empirical check: in Alavés' 10 home games, they won ≥4 corners in 8/10. Villarreal away won ≤4 in all 11 away games. The only scenario where Villarreal wins the count is if Villarreal win 4 and Alavés win ≤3 — which would require Alavés to fall to their single worst home performance (1 corner vs Espanyol) while Villarreal exceed their away maximum. Empirically, P(Alavés home wins ≤3 corners) = 2/10 = 20%, P(Villarreal away wins ≥4) = 1/11 = 9%. For Villarreal to win the spread, these need to coincide — roughly 2%.

But we must be more honest about correlation: if Villarreal dominate (4 corners) this may partially coincide with Alavés being suppressed. Still, the probability of Villarreal winning the corner count is low.

**My estimate: 73% base case, range 57–80%. Using 70% as a conservative, risk-adjusted figure.**

**Edge: 70% − 54.6% = +15.4%.**

### Caveats

1. **New manager effect**: Quique Sánchez Flores is 0-of-1 as Alavés boss. His tactical setup at home is unknown. A highly defensive 4-4-2 could depress Alavés's corner generation below 4 — this is the primary tail risk for picks 1 and 3.

2. **Villarreal absence stack**: Foyth, Moreno, Logan Costa, potentially Ayoze and Navarro all out or doubtful. If multiple senior players miss the trip, Villarreal could be more cautious and further reduce their corner output — which reinforces all three picks — or conversely a badly depleted side could panic and concede more corners than expected.

3. **Small spread sample**: The DK spread market has no movement reference and 8.1% overround. The Skellam model assumes independent Poisson processes; in reality both teams' corner counts can move together in a very open or very cagey game. H2H total of 7 in two of three games supports the cagey scenario, which would suppress Alavés corners and make the −0.5 bet feel precarious even if they ultimately win 4–2.

4. **Score-state risk**: If Villarreal concede an early goal (not implausible for a Alavés side motivated by relegation fear), game-flow reverses: Villarreal push forward and can accumulate corners, Alavés defend. This would boost Villarreal's corner output above the 2.8 central estimate and could lift the total above 9.5. The probability of Alavés scoring first is well below 50% given the quality gap, but it is a real scenario.

5. **Model confidence**: Poisson assumes independence across time within a match; corner counts are influenced by score-state momentum, which creates clustering. Given Villarreal's consistent sub-3 away corner profile, the model is robust for the direction of the prediction even if the exact probability is uncertain. All three picks pass the sensitivity test: they remain value at materially worse assumptions than the base case.
